Work · Case study

Volunteer manager at a three-day festival for 25,000 people.

Juneteenth Lake County is a parade and festival run by the Greater Waukegan Development Coalition. In 2024, its first year at Foss Park in North Chicago, I was one of the volunteer managers: the people on the ground who make sure volunteers know where to be and vendors can open on time.

Role
Volunteer manager
When
June 14–16, 2024, Foss Park, North Chicago
Organiser
Greater Waukegan Development Coalition
Areas
Community · Leadership

The event

Juneteenth Lake County celebrates the end of slavery in the United States with a parade from Waukegan High School and a three-day festival: live music from jazz to hip-hop, food, artisans, and booths for Black-owned businesses. The organisers say it draws more than 25,000 visitors from across the Chicago area each year.

After three years at Waukegan Harbor, the 2024 festival moved to Foss Park in North Chicago, a new site with more parking and new logistics for everyone involved.

My part

  • Coordinating volunteers on the ground: who is where, when shifts change, what to do when the plan meets the day.
  • Helping vendors get set up, from finding their space to getting them open and running.
  • Being the person a volunteer or a vendor could find when something was not working.

What I learned

An event is a design problem with people in it. A festival for 25,000 people runs on a few hundred small decisions made quickly by people who know what the plan is. Most of the job was making sure they did.
