Work · Case study

A Black Student Union that belongs to no single school.

The National Independent Black Student Union exists so that Black students across Lake County can find each other without waiting for a school to organise it. I have been its president since sophomore year. We have run more than sixty events.

Role
President, grades 10–12
Reach
Students from four Lake County districts
Events
60+
Areas
Community · Leadership

Why independent

A school-based BSU can only reach its own building. Lake County has several districts a few miles apart, and Black students in each of them were having the same conversations separately. Organising outside any single school meant we could bring them into one room, and it meant the group answered to its members rather than to a schedule set for us.

What we run

  • Kickoffs at the start of each year, in rented spaces where students from different schools actually meet: bowling alleys, restaurants, places people want to be.
  • Large meetings with invited speakers. State representatives and senators have come to talk with students about how the state works, what is open to them, and how to get involved.
  • Regular meetings, service and celebrations through the year. More than sixty events so far, each one planned, funded, promoted and run by students.

My part

As president I set the calendar, book the venues, invite and host the speakers, run the meetings, and make sure that a student who showed up once is welcomed back the second time. I also handle the part nobody sees: the budget, the permissions, the follow-up.

What I learned

Sixty events is sixty rooms to fill. People come back when they were welcomed the first time, and the second event is the one that decides whether there is a third. I also learned to ask. A state senator will come and speak to high-school students if someone writes a clear email and follows up.
