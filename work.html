  1. Warren Township High School’27Class website

    What it is

    The central site for the Warren Township High School Class of 2027. Public pages cover events with RSVPs and add-to-calendar links, announcements, deadlines, yearbook and graduation information, a photo gallery, senior spotlights and an opt-in senior directory. Students can submit photos, request announcements and nominate classmates through moderated forms.

    Everything is managed from a built-in admin dashboard with separate roles for editors, photographers and moderators, so other people can help run it.

    My part

    I designed and built the site, and I run it alongside the class’s social presence and senior events.

    What I took from it

    The hard part was not the code. It was deciding what the class actually needed, keeping information accurate, and building the moderation and roles that let other students contribute safely.

    • Next.js
    • TypeScript
    • Tailwind CSS
    • SQLite
    • Docker on Railway

  3. Identity, operations, toolsAVNTBrand

    What it is

    AVNT began as an identity, a monogram and a wordmark, and grew into a small operation that supports online project teams. To keep it organised I built an internal dashboard: a task and question board, a contacts directory, a Discord server builder, a shared ban list, a staff directory, an automatically generated activity log and a quick-links hub, all connected to a Discord bot.

    My part

    Founder. I designed the identity and built the software behind it.

    What I took from it

    A brand is mostly the routines behind it. Giving a second person their own account and their own log taught me more about running something than the logo did.

  6. Online communityOrganised around a game

    What it is

    An online community organised around a football game on Roblox. Beyond the game itself, it meant coordinating a team, keeping a brand consistent, running social channels and working with partners, the same organising work as any club, done at a distance.

    My part

    Project management, brand, social media and partnerships.

    What I took from it

    People join for the game and stay for each other. Most of the job was making the community somewhere worth staying: clear rules, consistent presence, and people who felt seen.
